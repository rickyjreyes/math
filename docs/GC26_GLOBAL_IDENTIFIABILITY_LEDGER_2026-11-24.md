## Rank versus global inverse

The square x↦x² has derivative 2x nonzero on an open dense subset, but x and −x collide. Even everywhere full column rank fails: x↦(x²,x³−x) has derivative (2x,3x²−1), never zero, while ±1 have the same image. For C¹ maps, full column rank implies local neighborhood injectivity at that point (choose a nonsingular square minor). Correct sufficient global statements: (i) C¹ square map on ℝᵈ with everywhere nonsingular derivative and properness is a global diffeomorphism (Hadamard); (ii) on a convex domain, a C¹ square map whose symmetric derivative part is positive definite everywhere is strictly monotone by integration along segments, hence injective; (iii) a proper injective continuous map is a topological embedding in Euclidean spaces. Do not confuse local injectivity with uniqueness on distant branches, or rank with conditioning. Exact collisions preclude unique recovery even with noiseless data; on a regular restricted slice practical stability requires a quantitative lower singular-value or inverse modulus bound.
