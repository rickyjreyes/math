# GC11: Can nonlinear dynamics select a unique wavelength?

**Plain-language question:** A linear instability can favor a scale, but does nonlinear pattern evolution settle on exactly one wavelength?

**Status:** Candidate classification/refutation problem; related band-selection and stability literature exists. *Unique selection is not presumed true.*

[Back to the catalogue](../README.md) · [Original statement](../FORMAL_PROBLEMS.md#gc11-nonlinear-finite-wavelength-selection)

## Fix the distinction between spaces and notions of selection

The original problem refers to periodic patterns on $\mathbb R^2$. Such a nonzero periodic pattern is **not** an element of $H^2(\mathbb R^2)$. Work instead with periodic profiles in $H^s_{\mathrm{per}}$ on a specified lattice, or with periodic entire-space solutions plus localized/Bloch–Floquet perturbations, and declare which.

An illustrative pattern-forming evolution is the Swift–Hohenberg equation

\`\`\`math
\partial_t u=\mu u-(1+\Delta)^2u-u^3.
\`\`\`

For the original fourth-order cubic–quintic Schrödinger equation, an analogous study must account for Hamiltonian rather than dissipative dynamics. **Results from the two models are not interchangeable.**

## Exact research question

For one frozen model, parameter range, geometry, and stability topology, determine the set $\mathcal K_{\mathrm{stab}}$ of wavenumbers of nonconstant stable periodic equilibria (or relative equilibria in a Hamiltonian model). Is this set a singleton, a nontrivial band, empty, or dependent on initial/boundary data?

For any claim of dynamic wavelength selection, define the initial-data class and convergence observable, then prove the chosen wavenumber is independent of initial conditions **or explicitly exhibit two different selected limits**.

## A likely overclaim to test

Linear dispersion having a unique minimum or maximum does **not** imply that nonlinear dynamics select a unique final wavenumber. Existing Swift–Hohenberg literature discusses ranges of stable stripe wavenumbers and wavelength selection by interfaces or parameter jumps.

## First falsifiable milestones

1. Freeze a one-dimensional stripe family on a fixed periodic cell, specifying whether the physical period may vary.
2. Derive the linearized Bloch/Floquet spectral stability criterion and find a parameter slice with more than one stable stripe wavenumber, or prove uniqueness under added conditions.
3. Distinguish energetic preference, spectral stability, nonlinear orbital stability, and dynamical attractor selection.
4. Check any proposed nonlinear theorem against the existing [exact positive-wave-number minimum](../../papers/exact-positive-wavenumber-minimum/main.tex) and [Lean dispersion result](../../lean/MathProofs/DispersionMinimum.lean), which address related but narrower spectral questions.

## Initial literature leads

- Scheel and Weinburd (2018), [*Wavenumber selection via spatial parameter jump*](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2017.0191).
- Knobloch, Uecker and Wetzel (2019), [*Defectlike structures and localized patterns in the cubic-quintic-septic Swift–Hohenberg equation*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.100.012204).

**Resolution:** A complete description of the stable-wavenumber set under explicit hypotheses, and a selection theorem or counterexample for precisely defined dynamics. No new physics is inferred merely from a selected linear wave number.
