## Canonical mathematical statement

**Model freeze.** On $L^2(\mathbb R^2)$, $H_0=(\Delta+1)^2$ with domain $H^4$. Fix $w(x)=\langle x\rangle^{-3}$, $0<\kappa<1$, $R_\kappa=(H_0+\kappa)^{-1}$, $N(\kappa)=\|wR_\kappa w\|_{L^2\to L^2}$.

**Precise mathematical target / proven narrower result.** For this **free** shifted bi-Laplacian, the sharp growth exponent is $p=1/2$: there are $0<c<C<\infty$ with $c\kappa^{-1/2}\le N(\kappa)\le C\kappa^{-1/2}$ for sufficiently small $\kappa>0$. For $h=wf$, the quadratic form is $\int |\widehat h(\xi)|^2/((1-|\xi|^2)^2+\kappa)\,d\xi$. Since $xw$ is bounded, $\widehat h\in H^1_\xi$; uniform Fourier-circle trace estimates bound the shell integral above by $C\kappa^{-1/2}\|f\|_2^2$, with the complement bounded. Choose a compactly supported f with nonzero Fourier trace of wf on $|\xi|=1$ to obtain the matching lower bound. Hence $\lim_{\kappa\downarrow0}\log N(\kappa)/\log(1/\kappa)=1/2$.

**Original scope text (preserved for comparison):**

Let $H_0=(\Delta+1)^2$ on $L^2(\mathbb R^2)$. For $0<\kappa<1$, set $R_\kappa=(H_0+\kappa)^{-1}$, $w(x)=\langle x\rangle^{-3}$, and $N(\kappa)=\|wR_\kappa w\|_{L^2\to L^2}$. Determine the exact growth exponent $p=\lim_{\kappa\downarrow0}\log N(\kappa)/\log(1/\kappa)$, if it exists, and sharp two-sided bounds for small kappa.
