# GC143: Causal propagation in dispersive field theories

**Status:** Known spreading fact; refine quantitative tails.
**Machine registry:** candidate (unchanged). **Audit type:** individual mathematical scoping review, not independent peer review or a Lean-checked new result.
**Sources:** [Existing scoped mathematical target](../scoped/GC143.md) · [Original GC page](../problems/05-higher-derivative-fields/GC143.md) · [Six-step standard](../PROBLEM_STANDARD.md) · [Review queue](REVIEW_QUEUE.md)

## Canonical mathematical statement

**Model freeze.** Take $H_0=(\Delta+1)^2$ with domain $H^4(\mathbb R^2)$, $U(t)=e^{-itH_0}$ and nonzero $u_0\in C_c^\infty(B_1;\mathbb C)$. For $t\ne0$ set $T(R,t,u_0)=\|\mathbf1_{\{|x|>R\}}U(t)u_0\|_2$.

**Precise mathematical target / proven narrower result.** Prove that $U(t)u_0$ cannot be compactly supported for $t\ne0$, using Paley–Wiener growth of the quartic Fourier phase. The potentially new question is the sharp envelope $\sup_{\|u_0\|_{H^s}\le1,\ \operatorname{supp}u_0\subset B_1,\ t\in[t_0,t_1]}T(R,t,u_0)$ for prescribed $s>0$ and $0<t_0<t_1$, as $R\to\infty$.

**Original scope text (preserved for comparison):**

For nonzero $u_0\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$, set $u(t)=e^{-it(\Delta+1)^2}u_0$. Decide whether $u(t,\cdot)$ can have compact spatial support for any $t\ne0$. Independently classify the sharp weighted off-support estimates $\|\mathbf1_{|x|>R}u(t)\|_2$ for fixed t and large R.

## Resolution criterion

Separate the classical qualitative no-finite-propagation proof from two-sided quantitative tails for the fully specified data class.

**Proof standard:** Give all quantifiers, functional spaces, parameter ranges, symmetries, boundary conditions, and the appropriate affirmative proof or explicit admissible negative witness. ## Closest known results

- [Paley–Wiener theorem](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Paley-Wiener_theorem): Classical exponential-type obstruction, applied to the exact quartic multiplier.
- [Li, Soffer and Yao, fourth-order Schrödinger decay](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2022.109816): Related but different dispersion.

**Novelty boundary:** The references support the stated classical or related results, not a globally complete novelty search. Any new claim needs focused source-to-statement comparison, and any proposed analytic derivation above needs independent checking.

## Falsifiable first milestones

1. Use the multiplier $e^{-it(1-|\xi|^2)^2}$ to prove that two compact supports at distinct times force zero data.
2. Prove one explicit class-uniform off-support upper bound.
3. Construct lower-tail examples ruling out an overly strong universal decay rate.
4. Compare the model and theorem quantifiers with the closest cited source; document any genuinely missing result before novelty or closure is claimed.

## Notes / remaining modeling decisions

Compactly supported C-infinity data without a bounded norm or Gevrey class do not define one universal sharp tail exponent.

**Existing audit (P0*):** Distinguish finite support propagation from dispersive off-cone estimates; a generic fourth-order spatial dispersive law has no finite propagation speed.
