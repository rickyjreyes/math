## Canonical mathematical statement

**Model freeze.** Fix the round $S^3,S^2$, normalized smooth area form $\omega$ with $\int_{S^2}\omega=1$, smooth $n:S^3\to S^2$, $F=n^*\omega$, $E(n)=\int_{S^3}(|dn|^2+|F|^2)\,dV$, $Q(n)=\int_{S^3}A\wedge F\in\mathbb Z$ for $dA=F$. Set $I_Q=\inf_{Q(n)=Q}E(n)$.

**Precise mathematical target / proven narrower result.** Classify the large-$|Q|$ asymptotic of $I_Q$, with sharp constants if available. Since the coexact primitive obeys $\|A\|_2\le C\|F\|_2$, the exact model already satisfies $|Q|\le C\|F\|_2^2$ and $I_Q\ge c|Q|$. Consequently an asserted **upper** bound $I_Q=O(|Q|^{3/4})$ contradicts this fixed compact $S^3$ model; that exponent cannot be imported from a different domain or normalization.

**Original scope text (preserved for comparison):**

Let $S^3$ have its standard unit round metric, and $S^2$ its unit round metric. Let $\omega\in\Omega^2(S^2)$ be the smooth positively oriented area form **normalized by** $\int_{S^2}\omega=1$. For every smooth $n:S^3\to S^2$, define
$$
F_n=n^*\omega,\qquad
E(n)=\int_{S^3}\bigl(|dn|^2+|F_n|^2\bigr)\,dV.
$$
Since $H^2(S^3)=0$, choose a one-form $A_n$ with $dA_n=F_n$. Define the Hopf integer $Q(n)=\int_{S^3}A_n\wedge F_n\in\mathbb Z$, independent of the choice of primitive. For every integer Q let
$$
I_Q=\inf\{E(n):n\in C^\infty(S^3,S^2),\ Q(n)=Q\}.
$$
Every Q is represented by some smooth map because $\pi_3(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z$.
