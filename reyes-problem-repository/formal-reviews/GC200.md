## Canonical mathematical statement

**Model freeze.** Let $T=\mathbb R/L_x\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R/L_y\mathbb Z$, $L_x,L_y>0$, and $u\in H^1(T;S^1)$ with winding $(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2$. Minimize $E(u)=\frac12\int_T|\nabla u|^2$.

**Precise mathematical target / proven narrower result.** The exact answer is $\min_{[u]=(m,n)}E(u)=2\pi^2(m^2L_y/L_x+n^2L_x/L_y)$. Every fixed-winding representative admits $u=e^{i[2\pi(mx/L_x+ny/L_y)+f]}$ with periodic real $f\in H^1(T)$; integration removes the affine-gradient cross term, yielding $E(u)=E_{\min}+\frac12\int_T|\nabla f|^2$. Minimizers are the harmonic phase representatives, unique up to a constant phase.

**Original scope text (preserved for comparison):**

For $L_x,L_y>0$, $T=\mathbb R/L_x\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R/L_y\mathbb Z$, and $u\in H^1(T;S^1)$ with winding pair $(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2$, minimize $E(u)=\tfrac12\int_T|\nabla u|^2dx$. Prove or refute $\min E=2\pi^2(m^2L_y/L_x+n^2L_x/L_y)$ and uniqueness of minimizers modulo constant phase for representatives $u=e^{2\pi i(mx/L_x+ny/L_y)}$.
