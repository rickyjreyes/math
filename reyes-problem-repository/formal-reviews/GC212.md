## Canonical mathematical statement

**Model freeze.** Given $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$, suppose almost every sample is $n_\omega\in C([0,T],C(S^3,S^2))$, with uniform spatial topology. Let $Q(n)\in\mathbb Z$ denote the Hopf invariant.

**Precise mathematical target / proven narrower result.** For every such sample, $Q(n_\omega(t))=Q(n_\omega(0))$ for all $t\in[0,T]$, since $n_\omega$ itself is a homotopy. Hence the prescribed continuous nonvanishing model cannot exhibit stochastic topological charge change. A substantive extension must specify an SPDE or random map valued outside $S^2$, and precisely define the first time when continuity, nonvanishing, or required spatial regularity is lost.

**Original scope text (preserved for comparison):**

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ be any probability space and $n_\omega\in C([0,T],C(S^3,S^2))$ for almost every ω. Determine whether $Q(n_\omega(t))=Q(n_\omega(0))$ for all t almost surely. For random paths defined only on $[0,\tau(\omega))$, determine the necessary kind of loss of continuity before the integer can change.

## Notes / remaining modeling decisions

Loss of continuity is necessary for a charge change only relative to the stated map space. A field can remain continuous as an (mathbb R^3)-valued function yet cross zero and lose its (S^2)-normalization.

**Existing audit (P0*):** If sample paths are jointly continuous and never vanish, topological charge is homotopy-invariant pathwise; make zero hitting and regularity loss the substantive problem.
