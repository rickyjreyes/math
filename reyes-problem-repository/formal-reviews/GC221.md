**Precise source target, reproduced without changing mathematical quantifiers:**

Let $f(x)=2x\bmod1$ on $[0,1)$, and $b_T(x)=\lfloor2f^T(x)\rfloor\in\{0,1\}$. An **exact-prefix oracle** supplies the first p binary digits of a non-dyadic x. Determine the minimum p(T) sufficient to output $b_T(x)$ for every x with the supplied prefix. **Separately**, for an approximate-real oracle returning rational $\widetilde x$ with $|x-\widetilde x|\le2^{-p}$, impose the margin promise $\operatorname{dist}(f^T(x),\{0,1/2,1\})\ge\gamma>0$, and derive a sufficient p(T,\gamma).

**Requested decision.** Determine whether the exact quantified claim above holds for the specified admissible class. If it holds, identify the strongest necessary and sufficient conditions available in the frozen model; if not, construct a counterexample or prove an obstruction with explicitly stated parameters, topology and norm. This restatement does not broaden the source target or establish its novelty.

## Resolution criterion

**Acceptance / disproof condition.** For an exact-prefix oracle, p=T+1 exactly and no margin promise is needed: the (T+1)st digit determines $b_T$. An approximate-value oracle has a different information model, and without a margin finite absolute-error approximations cannot always distinguish inputs across a decision boundary.

A complete answer must give a theorem with all domains, parameters, quantifiers, function spaces, boundary/initial data and the relevant topology, plus a proof or an admissible counterexample. Identify known theorems first; avoid treating a classical consequence as novel. An exactly corresponding Lean 4 declaration and axiom/CI audit would be valuable when nontrivial but is not automatically required to state or prove an ordinary mathematical fact.
