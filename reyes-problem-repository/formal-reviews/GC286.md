## Canonical mathematical statement

**Model freeze.** For one correct N-gate operation, define nonnegative joule contributions $E_{\rm src},E_{\rm mod},E_{\rm det},E_{\rm cool},E_{\rm mem},E_{\rm idle}$, and reference energy $E_{\rm ref}>0$ for the same input/output task. Let $E_{\rm opt}$ be the sum. With $E_{\rm src}=N_{\rm ph}h\nu/\eta_{\rm wall}$, fix $h\nu>0$, $0<\eta_{\rm wall}\le1$.

**Precise mathematical target / proven narrower result.** The exact advantage condition is $E_{\rm opt}<E_{\rm ref}$, an accounting identity. Define $E_{\rm other}=E_{\rm mod}+E_{\rm det}+E_{\rm cool}+E_{\rm mem}+E_{\rm idle}$. If $E_{\rm ref}\le E_{\rm other}$, no nonnegative photon count suffices; otherwise $N_{\rm ph}<\eta_{\rm wall}(E_{\rm ref}-E_{\rm other})/(h\nu)$. The nontrivial problem is to measure or tightly bound each term under a *matched* workload and error rate.

**Original scope text (preserved for comparison):**

For one completed N-gate logical operation, define measured source energy E_src, modulator/control energy E_mod, detector energy E_det, cooling energy E_cool, memory energy E_mem and idle baseline E_idle, all nonnegative joules per completed operation. Let E_opt=sum of these six quantities, and choose an electronic reference performing the **same input/output function** with measured energy E_ref>0. Prove that optical energy advantage holds iff E_src+E_mod+E_det+E_cool+E_mem+E_idle<E_ref; when E_src=N_ph hν/η_wall, derive the maximal permitted N_ph as a function of other terms and efficiency 0<η_wall≤1.
