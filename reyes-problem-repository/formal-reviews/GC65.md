**Precise source target, reproduced without changing mathematical quantifiers:**

Let $u_t=\mu u-(1+\Delta)^2u-u^3$ on the square torus $\mathbb T^2=(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2$, with $\mu=2$, real initial data $H^2$. For each integer $N\ge1$, let $P_N$ be the $L^2$-orthogonal projection onto Fourier modes $|k|\le N$, and $Q_N=I-P_N$. Determine whether **there exist** $N$ and a globally Lipschitz $\Phi:P_NH^2\to Q_NH^2$ such that $\mathcal M=\{p+\Phi(p)\}$ is forward invariant and, for every bounded subset $B\subset H^2$, there are $C_B,\lambda>0$ with $\operatorname{dist}_{H^2}(S(t)B,\mathcal M)\le C_B e^{-\lambda t}$ for all $t\ge0$.

If it holds, identify the strongest necessary and sufficient conditions available in the frozen model; if not, construct a counterexample or prove an obstruction with explicitly stated parameters, topology and norm. ## Resolution criterion

**What answers it.** A proof must exhibit N, Phi, invariance and exponential tracking. A disproof must exclude every N under these precise choices. The finite-dimensional attractor alone is insufficient.

A complete answer must give a theorem with all domains, parameters, quantifiers, function spaces, boundary/initial data and the relevant topology, plus a proof or an admissible counterexample. Identify known theorems first; avoid treating a classical consequence as novel. An exactly corresponding Lean 4 declaration and axiom/CI audit would be valuable when nontrivial but is not automatically required to state or prove an ordinary mathematical fact.
