**Precise source target, reproduced without changing mathematical quantifiers:**

For each nonzero real $\phi\in H^4(\mathbb R^2)$ satisfying $F_\omega(\phi)=0$, define $\mathcal A_\phi$ as in W. Call an internal mode an $L^2$ eigenvector of $\mathcal A_\phi$ at $i\nu$, $0<\nu<1$, isolated from the essential spectrum. Determine whether **there exist** such $(\omega,\phi,\nu)$ and an $H^2$-open perturbation class of solutions whose Riesz-mode projection amplitude tends to zero as $t\to+\infty$.

**Proposed corrected nonvacuous benchmark (not substituted into the historical source):** Fix λ∈(0,1/4) and a nonzero real profile φ∈H⁴(ℝ²) satisfying

$
(\Delta+1)^2\phi+\lambda\phi-\phi^3+\phi^5=0.
$

The linearization in real coordinates has blocks $L_+=(\Delta+1)^2+\lambda-3\phi^2+5\phi^4$ and $L_-=(\Delta+1)^2+\lambda-\phi^2+\phi^4$. Its essential spectrum is outside the central interval $(-i\lambda,i\lambda)$; an isolated positive internal mode must have $0<\nu<\lambda$. If a sufficiently regular nondegenerate branch and such a mode exist, ask for decay of its Riesz spectral projection for a rigorously specified family of small perturbations, with phase/translation modulation and possible unstable directions accounted for. The required existence and damping estimates are **not proved here**.
