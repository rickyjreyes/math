## Frozen model and inherited foundations

The original equation is i u_t = (Delta+k²)²u - beta |u|²u + gamma |u|⁴u
on H²(R²; C), k>=0, beta,gamma>0. A standing wave e^{i omega t} phi
solves the corresponding weak stationary equation with +omega phi.
Use the phase-translation orbit only, not a periodic domain or an orbit
enlarged by spatial rotations. Existing global_wellposed and conservation
supply the actual infinite-dimensional dynamics. Existing stable_core
covers the full minimizer set in the proved binding range. PR #97 gives
periodic linear spectral instability, not localized nonlinear instability;
PR #99 proposes extension of the set result to all masses at k=0.

## A. Isolated minimizer orbit theorem

Let G_m be the full minimizing set in the existing binding range, O the
phase-translation orbit of phi in G_m. Assume there is r>0 with
g in G_m and d(g,O)<r implying g in O. No uniqueness of G_m is assumed.

Given epsilon>0, take eta=min(r,epsilon)/8. Set stability and O subset G_m
give a delta so trajectories initially delta-close to O stay eta-close
to G_m. Also take delta<=eta. The distance to the nonempty orbit is
1-Lipschitz in the H² metric and continuous along the global flow.
If a trajectory leaves the 2 eta tube of O, there is a first crossing
of distance 2 eta. At that point choose g in G_m within eta of the
trajectory. Its orbit distance is <3 eta<r, hence g belongs to O.
This contradicts distance 2 eta. The uniform upper bound 2 eta<epsilon
proves the strict-sup stability definition, even for mass-changing data.

Candidate Lean: GC01IndividualOrbit.isolated_orbit_stability; its isolation
premise is a static property and does not assume stability. The case of a
unique minimizing orbit is included. Nonunique isolated orbits are allowed,
but no specific nonunique solution branch is proved to satisfy isolation.

Rotation obstruction: the model is rotation-invariant. If rotated
minimizers arbitrarily near phi do not belong to its phase-translation
orbit, the isolation hypothesis fails. This is not itself instability.
For sufficiently regular profiles, the infinitesimal rotation derivative
is another Hessian kernel vector when it exists in the operator domain
and is independent of phase and translation tangents. A vortex transforming
by phase under rotations is not automatically such a case.

## B. Static invariant barriers beyond minimizers

For any phi in H² put F(v)=|M(v)-M(phi)|+|E(v)-E(phi)|. This is constant
along the original flow, vanishes on O, and is uniformly small when the
initial orbit distance is small (bounded-set mass/energy Lipschitz
estimates, uniform coercivity bound on O).

If, for every epsilon>0, there exist 0<r<epsilon and a>0 such that
F(v)>=a whenever d(v,O)=r, then O is nonlinearly orbitally stable.
Choose initial distance less than r and F(u0)<a. Continuity forces a
crossing of radius r if the solution exits; conservation contradicts
the barrier. The proof uses no minimization or spectral equivalence.
The condition is sufficient, not necessary, and no concrete excited
profile is yet shown to satisfy it.

Candidate Lean: orbit_invariants, defect_small_near_orbit,
orbit_stability_of_barriers, continuous_distX_solution.
This establishes the dynamic implication conditional on static barriers,
not their existence for any specified excited standing wave.

## C. Exact model-specific dilation obstruction

Write D=||Delta phi||², K=||grad phi||², Q=||phi||_4^4, S=||phi||_6^6,
M=||phi||², P=D+(gamma/3)S and B=2k²K+(beta/2)Q.
For phi_lambda(x)=lambda phi(lambda x), M is constant and
E(phi_lambda)=lambda^4 P-lambda² B+k^4 M.
For the repository's IsStationary (virial identity), B=2P. Therefore

E(phi_lambda)-E(phi)=P(lambda²-1)².

P>0 for positive mass and gamma>0, hence strict positivity for every
lambda>0 other than 1, and second derivative at 1 is 8P>0.
This excludes this one negative constrained direction, not negative
directions in general. For weak PDE stationary solutions, a justified
virial identity (e.g. via localization) is a separate obligation;
the Lean claim uses precisely IsStationary, not an assumed identification
with all weak stationary solutions.

Candidate Lean: stationary_dilation_gap, stationary_dilation_strict.

## D. Actual whole-space Hessian and essential-spectrum obstruction
(analytical, not encoded in Lean here)

Work over the real Hilbert space of complex-valued functions; normalize
the action as (E+omega M)/2. At a stationary solution phi the derivative
of its gradient in a perturbation h is

L h = ((Delta+k²)²+omega) h + (-beta |phi|²+gamma |phi|⁴) h
      + (-2 beta+4 gamma |phi|²) Re(conj(phi) h) phi.

For real phi this splits into Lplus=A+omega-3 beta phi²+5 gamma phi⁴
and Lminus=A+omega-beta phi²+gamma phi⁴, A=(Delta+k²)².
Phase gives L(i phi)=0 and translations give L(partial_j phi)=0.

The polynomial nonlinearity maps H² to H² by the two-dimensional Sobolev
algebra estimate. The elliptic stationary equation then gives phi in H^6
using its Fourier multiplier at large frequencies; iteration gives
all finite Sobolev regularities. H² embeds in continuous functions
vanishing at spatial infinity, so all matrix multiplication potentials
in L are bounded and vanish at infinity. They are relatively compact
with respect to A on its H^4 domain: cut off in a bounded region,
apply local Rellich compactness, then bound the tail in operator norm
by the sup norm of the potential outside the cutoff.

The self-adjoint real two-component Hessian therefore has essential
spectrum [omega,infinity) by Weyl's theorem, since the free symbol is
(|xi|²-k²)²+omega. At omega>0 the negative Morse index is finite;
at omega<0 it is infinite (negative spectral subspace contains an
infinite-dimensional essential-spectrum sector below zero). At omega=0
zero lies in essential spectrum. In particular at omega<=0 there
cannot be a strictly positive H² coercivity bound after removing
only finitely many tangent/constraint directions: use a spectral
sequence at or below zero, then remove its finite-dimensional
projections. This is a statement about the Hessian, not about
nonlinear orbital instability.

Testing the stationary equation against phi also gives
T+omega M=beta Q-gamma S <= beta² M/(4 gamma), by pointwise completion
of a square. For nonzero phi, T>0: its Fourier transform cannot be
supported on the measure-zero shell |xi|=k. Thus omega<beta²/(4 gamma).
No positive-frequency lower bound is established here.

## E. Conditional Hessian and linear-to-nonlinear implications
(analytical, not encoded in Lean here)

If the action Hessian is uniformly positive in H² on the real
L²-mass tangent space orthogonal to the phase and translation tangents,
then adding a sufficiently large positive multiple of (M-M(phi))²
to the action yields positivity on the full normal complement.
Proof: decompose h in the normal complement into a mass-tangent
part and a component along phi, control the bounded bilinear cross
term by Young's inequality, and choose the penalty to dominate its
mass-direction contribution. A local modulation chart removes the
phase/translation tangents, and Taylor expansion yields a quadratic
lower bound for the conserved penalized action at small orbit distance.
It implies the static barriers of B (the penalty is bounded by a
constant times F when F<=1), hence nonlinear stability, including
non-minimizing local constrained minima. This requires actual coercivity,
not merely spectral stability or a sign of an unverified slope.

Conversely, suppose the rotating-frame linearized H² group has a real
growing eigenvector h with eigenvalue lambda>0 and a bound
||exp(t J L)||_{H²->H²}<=C exp(lambda t), t>=0, where J is multiplication
by -i. Assume the phase-translation orbit is locally a smooth
embedded submanifold at phi (for a nonzero smooth localized profile
the phase/translation tangent vectors are independent; L² translations
escape weakly at infinity). Since the tangent space is in the kernel,
h is transverse to it. For initial perturbation delta h, the exact
rotating-frame equation is w_t=JL w+N(w), with
||N(w)||_{H²}<=C_N||w||²_{H²} locally by the Sobolev algebra estimate.
Duhamel and the group bound, bootstrapping ||w(t)||<=2 delta exp(lambda t)
for unit h, give ||w(t)-delta exp(lambda t)h||<=C' delta² exp(2 lambda t)
up to T=(1/lambda)log(eta/delta), for fixed sufficiently small eta.
At T the local orbit distance is at least c eta-O(eta²)>0, since h
is transverse. Initial orbit distance tends to zero with delta.
This proves nonlinear orbital instability under the stated growth and
modulation hypotheses, not a general equivalence between spectral
and nonlinear instability. Complex eigenvalues, Jordan blocks,
sharp semigroup bounds and degenerate branches require further work.

GSS/Hamiltonian-Krein theory is a potential source of spectral count
conditions but neither the sign of a frequency slope alone nor a
negative Hessian direction establishes instability. A real excited
profile does not automatically have the spectral ordering of a
second-order positive ground state; fourth-order elliptic operators
lack the corresponding maximum principle in general. Required:
actual localized branch construction, index and kernel computation,
essential threshold treatment, resolvent/semigroup estimates and
nonlinear escape in H². No concrete non-minimizing stable or unstable
branch has been classified in this work.
