## A. All-mass real radial stationary profiles with infinitely many sign changes

Fix k=0, beta,gamma>0, m>0. Minimize the original energy E on the closed
real radial subspace Y of H²(R²;C) at mass m. A nonzero smooth real radial
bump normalized to m has negative energy after sufficiently small
mass-preserving dilation, as in the vortex ledger. The same coercivity,
radial H¹ tail estimate, Rellich argument, and constant-amplitude spatial
stretching argument in sections 1–2 of that ledger apply to Y. Specifically,
a weak limit has E(u)<=inf E<0 and a=M(u)>0; if a<m, v(x)=u(x/sqrt(m/a))
has mass m and E(v)=(m/a)E(u)-(m/a-a/m)D(u)<E(u), contradiction.
Hence attainment and strong H² compactness within Y.

The real radial subspace is the fixed point set of the compact group
generated by planar rotations and complex conjugation. Both preserve E,M
as real functionals. The mass derivative is nonzero on Y. The Lagrange
multiplier derivative therefore vanishes on Y, and averaging any real
H² test vector over that group extends its vanishing to all H². The profile
is a genuine full stationary solution, not a projection of the PDE:
Delta² f + omega f - beta f³ + gamma f⁵=0.
D-beta Q/4+gamma S/3=0 from the exact dilation formula and
D+omega m=beta Q-gamma S from testing against f give
omega m=3D+gamma S/3>0. Fourier elliptic bootstrapping gives H^s for every
finite s, and f and Delta f are continuous and vanish at infinity.

**Sign-change theorem.** In fact every nonzero real radial H² solution
at k=0, omega>0 changes sign arbitrarily far out. Write f(r) for its
smooth radial representative and g(r)=Delta f(r). For sufficiently large
r, W(r)=omega-beta f(r)^2+gamma f(r)^4 is strictly positive. Suppose f>=0
on a tail. The stationary equation gives (r g')'=-r W f<=0 there.
If r g' were negative at any tail radius R, it would remain bounded
above by that negative number, implying g(r)<=g(R)+R g'(R) log(r/R),
contrary to g(r)->0. Thus g'>=0, and g<=0 since g->0.
Now (r f')'=r g<=0. The identical logarithmic argument gives f'>=0
and hence f<=0 on the tail, since f->0. Thus f=0 there. Uniqueness of
the radial fourth-order ODE at any positive radius (smooth coefficients,
polynomial nonlinearity) gives f identically zero, contradiction.
Apply the same reasoning to -f to rule out an eventually nonpositive
tail. Continuity gives positive and negative values beyond every radius.

This establishes actual sign-changing continuum standing-wave existence
for every positive mass in the zero-shell model. Sign changes do NOT
identify excited states in a fourth-order problem: no comparison principle
orders these states by nodes, and this construction does not establish
non-minimality or stability in unrestricted H². The real radial minimizer
set is not necessarily invariant under the complex time evolution; do not
infer stability from real radial minimization.

## B. Exact angular blocks, arbitrary k and positive omega

Let phi=e^{i l theta} f(r) be any localized equivariant stationary state,
l integer, allowing complex f. Set s=|f|²,
P=-2 beta s+3 gamma s², C=(-beta+2 gamma s) f²,
Delta_j=d²/dr²+(1/r)d/dr-j²/r², B_j=(Delta_j+k²)²+omega+P.
Radial spaces and operator domains are inherited from the exact angular
subspaces of H⁴(R²), not arbitrary boundary conditions at r=0.
Differentiating the actual polynomial nonlinearity as a real map gives
Lh=((Delta+k²)²+omega+P)h + (-beta+2 gamma s) phi² conjugate(h).
In particular the pointwise real action Hessian has tangential and radial
potential eigenvalues -beta s+gamma s² and -3 beta s+5 gamma s².
The Hessian is self-adjoint on real L² with H⁴ domain; bounded potentials
decay at infinity. L's essential spectrum is [omega,infinity).
For the generator G=-iL, complexify the real Hilbert space. The free
generator is skew-adjoint and the potentials are relatively compact
H⁴->L² (local Rellich and small tails). Fredholm essential spectrum is
i(-infinity,-omega] union i[omega,infinity) for omega>0 (the same free
symbol range as in the individual-orbit ledger). Off the imaginary axis
G-z is Fredholm of index zero. For example sufficiently large |Re z|
gives invertibility by the bounded perturbation resolvent estimate;
analytic Fredholm then gives isolated off-axis eigenvalues of finite
algebraic multiplicity in each open half-plane.

For q>=1 write h=e^{i(l+q)theta} a(r)+e^{i(l-q)theta} conjugate(b(r)).
Then z=(a,b) evolves under the exact complex-linear generator
-i J_q H_q, where
    H_q = [[B_(l+q), C], [conjugate(C), B_(l-q)]],
    J_q = diag(1,-1).
Here the second equation comes from conjugating the equation for the
lower angular component. H_q is self-adjoint; this pairing is an exact
invariant real subspace of the original real-linear evolution.
The real generator complexification also contains the conjugate block.
No assumption that f is real or that higher angular modes are uncoupled
is made. The q=0 real-linear block is the equivariant sector X_l.

Phase i phi lies in q=0, translations partial_1 phi, partial_2 phi in
q=1, and rotation partial_theta phi=i l phi is already the phase
direction. For nonzero phi the phase and translation tangents are
linearly independent (a nontrivial constant-coefficient first-order
relation would constrain its Fourier support to a null set). Additional
kernel and generalized eigenvectors are not excluded. Rotation for a
non-equivariant profile is a kernel tangent only when its derivative
exists in the required domain; it need not be removed from the frozen
phase-translation orbit.

## C. Explicit finite angular cutoff for the full spectrum

Assume omega>0. Let C0=9 beta²/(20 gamma)>0. Completing squares in the
two pointwise potential eigenvalues proves V>=-C0 I at every x. Choose R
so that V>=-(omega/2) I outside |x|<=R; this is possible since phi->0.
For any h in the q>=1 angular pair set M=||h||², T=||(Delta+k²)h||²,
j=min(|l+q|,|l-q|). For j>0, angular orthogonality and the polar gradient
identity imply
    integral_{|x|<=R} |h|² <= R²/j² ||grad h||².
Integration by parts or Plancherel gives
    ||grad h||² <= k² M + sqrt(T M).
Using the pointwise lower bounds inside and outside yields
    <h,Lh> >= T + (omega/2)M - a(k² M + sqrt(T M)),
    a=C0 R²/j².
Young's inequality implies the concrete coercivity theorem:
    a k² + a²/2 <= omega/4
    ==> <h,Lh> >= T/2 + (omega/4) M.
For q>|l|, j=q-|l|, so every sufficiently large q meets the condition.
An explicit choice is
    A = sqrt(k^4+omega/2)-k² > 0,
    q >= |l| + R sqrt(C0/A), q>|l|.
This is an H² coercivity estimate (M+T equivalent to H² squared)
on the actual angular pair, not merely an approximate radial ansatz.

An off-axis eigenvector z of -i J_q H_q has zero H_q-energy:
from -i J_q H_q z=lambda z, take the Hermitian product with H_q z
and real parts; Re(lambda) <z,H_q z>=0.
Strict positivity contradicts z nonzero. The Fredholm property from
B, applied to the block (the free block is skew-adjoint and the
restricted potential is relatively compact), excludes all off-axis
spectrum in these high blocks, not just real eigenvalues. This does
not exclude imaginary internal modes, or assert nonlinear stability
of the vortex. For the full generator, an off-axis eigenvector has a
nonzero component in at least one invariant block (or conjugate block),
so no uniform direct-sum resolvent assertion is needed.

## D. The equivariant block of a constrained minimizer

For the X_l minimizers constructed in the earlier k=0 vortex ledger,
the real quadratic form of L is nonnegative on
T={h in X_l : Re <phi,h>=0}. Extend T and its form by complexification.
Every eigenvector z in the complexified q=0 sector with nonzero eigenvalue
lies in the complexified T: the mass differential annihilates G
since L(i phi)=0. If Re lambda != 0, the Hamiltonian energy identity
gives <z,Lz>=0. Positivity on T and polarization imply that Lz is in
the complexified real span of phi (T has real codimension one before
complexification). Therefore Gz=-iLz belongs to the complexified
phase tangent, whose generator image is zero. Since Gz=lambda z,
lambda!=0 forces z=0, contradiction. Fredholmness then excludes
off-axis spectrum in q=0. This does not require a strict constrained
Hessian, a nondegenerate branch, or isolation of the minimizing orbit.

Consequently for each of the actual non-minimizing X_l minimizers of
the earlier theorem (k=0, |l|>=2, every positive mass), unrestricted
spectral stability is equivalent to absence of off-axis eigenvalues
in the FINITE collection of paired radial operators q>=1 failing
the explicit high-mode cutoff. For other positive-frequency equivariant
stationary states q=0 must also be checked unless the constrained
nonnegativity is independently established. For q=1 exact translation
zero modes must not be counted as growth.

This is an exact finite-block criterion, not a computed spectral
classification: the remaining operators are still infinite-dimensional.
In the small-mass region the prior constrained index >=4|l| implies
many negative directions outside X_l, but neither this lower bound
nor the finite-block criterion decides whether these are negative
Krein-signature imaginary modes or off-axis eigenvalues. Even an
indefinite Hessian cannot in general be identified with exponential
growth: the finite Hamiltonian oscillator with negative definite
quadratic energy has purely imaginary spectrum and norm-preserving
flow. That example is a logical counterexample, not a PDE vortex.

## E. What remains and verification boundary

- Continuum angular compactness and sign-change proof, Hessian identification,
  operator-domain/Fredholm arguments, spectral exclusion, and q=0 proof
  are analytical here, not Lean-certified.
- The candidate Lean module GC01SpectralReduction encodes the actual
  nonlinear action's exact central polynomial difference, its shifted
  sum-of-squares identity and uniform potential lower bound, quantitative
  high-angular-mode coercivity algebra, paired coefficients, and the
  finite-dimensional counterexample. Exact roots are audited. No continuum
  hypothesis is disguised as a formal theorem.
- Prove low-block eigenvalue existence/nonexistence on concrete branches,
  including negative Krein modes, kernel multiplicities and threshold
  resonances. An Evans function or validated spectral enclosure must
  control radial infinity and the correct origin domain.
- Prove nonlinear escape for genuine unstable eigenmodes with the
  needed H² semigroup bound, or coercive invariant barriers including
  all degeneracies for stable non-minimizing profiles. Spectral stability
  alone does not prove orbital stability.
- k!=0 vortex existence and non-minimality, classification of real
  sign-changing, excited and multi-profile states, and omega<=0 embedded
  spectral regimes are unresolved. Keep GC01 #54 and #80 open.

  numerical stability, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12636817/ (2003);
