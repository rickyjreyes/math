# GC01 follow-up: localized vortex standing waves and constrained Morse-index bounds

2026-11-24. Analytical theorem for the exact k=0 GC01 equation, with
candidate Lean scalar estimates only. No claim of unrestricted nonlinear
orbital instability or kernel verification of the continuum theorem.

## Theorem

Fix beta,gamma>0, mass m>0 and an integer l with |l|>=2. For k=0
there exists a nonzero standing-wave profile phi in H²(R²;C) of the
form phi(r,theta)=exp(i l theta) f(r), satisfying the original weak
stationary equation with a frequency omega>0. It is a constrained
minimizer in its angular-equivariant subspace but is NOT a global
mass-constrained energy minimizer.

If
    gamma sqrt(3 beta²/(32 gamma)) m < 2 beta
(equivalently m < 8 sqrt(2/(3 gamma))), then the real constrained
Hessian of (E+omega M)/2 has Morse index at least 4|l|.
In particular this vortex is not even a local constrained minimum.
The unrestricted nonlinear orbital stability of these vortices is
not determined by this theorem. The negative Morse index lower bound
must not be identified with a positive-real-part eigenvalue count.

## 1. Existence in the closed equivariant space

Write X_l for the closed complex linear subspace of H² fixed by the
isometric circle action u(x) -> exp(-i l alpha) u(R_alpha x).
Its elements have the stated polar form a.e. Let J_l(m) be the
infimum of the exact k=0 Hamiltonian on X_l at mass m.

Nonemptiness: choose a smooth nonzero radial bump supported in an
annulus, multiply by exp(i l theta), normalize its L² mass. Along the
mass-preserving dilates u_lambda(x)=lambda u(lambda x), its energy is
lambda^4 (D+gamma S/3) - lambda² beta Q/2, negative for small positive
lambda. The pointwise energy lower bound from F01 gives J_l(m)>-infty
and coercivity of every minimizing sequence in H².

A bounded sequence in X_l has a weakly convergent H² subsequence
with limit in X_l. Its amplitudes |u_n| are radial H¹ functions with
uniform H¹ bound by the weak diamagnetic inequality. The radial H¹
estimate in dimension two gives |u_n(x)| <= C |x|^(-1/2) a.e. away
from the origin. Consequently for p=4,6 the exterior p-integral is
bounded by (C R^(-1/2))^(p-2) m for |x|>R, uniformly. Local Rellich
compactness on balls, followed by a diagonal subsequence and this
tail estimate, gives strong L⁴ and L⁶ convergence to the weak limit u.
Thus E(u)<=J_l(m)<0 by weak lower semicontinuity of D. In particular
u is nonzero and its mass a lies in (0,m].

If a<m, set t=m/a>1 and v(x)=u(x/sqrt(t)), which is still in X_l.
Then M(v)=m and
    E(v)=t E(u) - (t-1/t) D(u) <= t E(u) < E(u) <= J_l(m),
a contradiction. Hence a=m and u attains J_l(m). This also proves
strong H² subsequential compactness: convergence of mass and of
energy together with strong L⁴/L⁶ convergence forces convergence
of D, and the Fourier norm M+D is equivalent to the H² norm.

No assumption of unrestricted minimizer existence, binding or
uniqueness is used. This construction actually works for every
integer l, including 0 and 1; the non-minimizer proof below is
restricted to |l|>=2.

## 2. Full PDE and frequency

The functionals E and M are continuously real differentiable on H²
by the two-dimensional algebra estimate. Since M'(u) is nonzero in
X_l (test against u), the multiplier theorem gives omega such that
(E+omega M)' vanishes on X_l. For arbitrary h in H² average its orbit
under the circle action. The average belongs to X_l; invariance of
E,M and of u gives the derivative on h equal to that on the average.
Therefore the stationary weak equation holds on ALL H² test functions:
    Delta² u + omega u - beta |u|²u + gamma |u|⁴u = 0.
The standing wave exp(i omega t)u solves the original whole-space
evolution, which is globally well-posed by F01.

No differentiation of the H² dilation curve itself is needed:
the exact scalar energy formula attains a minimum at lambda=1.
It yields D-beta Q/4+gamma S/3=0. Testing the stationary equation
against u gives D+omega m=beta Q-gamma S, and hence
    omega m=3D+gamma S/3>0.
Also E(u)=-D-gamma S/3<0. Elliptic Fourier regularity gives u in
H^s for every finite s by bootstrapping the polynomial nonlinearity.

## 3. Strict angular kinetic comparison

Without loss l>=2 (conjugation covers negative charges). For a smooth
compactly supported angular-equivariant profile u_l=exp(i l theta)f(r),
Taylor expansion at the origin gives f=O(r^l), f'=O(r^(l-1)).
For integer j with |j|<=l, put u_j=exp(i j theta)f(r).
These functions belong to H²: polar derivatives through order two
are locally square integrable, and the boundary terms at r=0 vanish.

Define
    A = 2 pi integral_0^infty |f'|² dr/r,
    B = 2 pi integral_0^infty |f|² dr/r³.
Direct expansion of D_j = 2 pi integral |f''+f'/r-j² f/r²|² r dr
and integration by parts gives
    D_j = D_0 + 2 j² A + j²(j²-4) B,
    D_l-D_j = (l²-j²)[2 A+(l²+j²-4)B].
Indeed Re integral (f''+f'/r) conj(f) dr/r =
-integral |f'|² dr/r + 2 integral |f|² dr/r³.
All boundary terms vanish by the origin estimate and compact support.

For |j|<=l the coefficient comparison is nonnegative since l²>=4.
It follows that the map u_l -> u_j is bounded from X_l with the H²
norm M+D into H². Smooth compactly supported equivariant functions
are dense in X_l: approximate by smooth compactly supported functions,
then average over the isometric circle action. The comparison thus
extends to X_l. More explicitly, on differences of approximants the
j=0 identity D_l-D_0=2l² A+l²(l²-4)B controls the A norm; the B norm
is controlled by A via weighted Hardy
    integral |f|²/r³ <= integral |f'|²/r
(obtained by integration by parts and Cauchy-Schwarz). The weighted
integrals therefore converge as well, yielding the exact difference
identity for the limiting profile. Nonzero f has A>0: zero weighted
derivative would force a constant f, which cannot have finite nonzero
mass on R².

All u_j have identical M,Q,S. For |j|<l, D_j<D_l. Taking j=0 gives
    I(m) <= E(u_0) < E(u_l)=J_l(m).
This is a concrete non-minimizing stationary wave of the original PDE
for each prescribed positive mass and each charge |l|>=2.

## 4. Negative constrained Hessian subspace

For a stationary complex phi, write the real action Hessian form as
    H_phi[h] = ||Delta h||² + omega ||h||²
      + integral (-beta |phi|²+gamma |phi|⁴)|h|²
      + integral (-2 beta+4 gamma |phi|²)(Re(conj(phi) h))².
This is the second derivative of (E+omega M)/2, with the real inner
product. The mass tangent consists of h satisfying Re <phi,h> = 0.

Assume l>=2, and choose arbitrary complex coefficients c_j for
j=-l,-l+1,...,l-1. Set
    h(r,theta)=f(r) sum_j c_j exp(i j theta).
This is a real 4l-dimensional subspace in H² and is mass-tangent by
angular orthogonality. The angular average of |sum_j c_j exp(i j theta)|²
is sum_j |c_j|². Also all shifted frequencies j-l are strictly negative,
so the angular average of the square of the real part of
sum_j c_j exp(i(j-l)theta) is (1/2) sum_j |c_j|².
No opposite shifted frequencies occur, so no cross terms survive.

Consequently the exact form restricted to this subspace is
    H_phi[h] = sum_j |c_j|² (D_j+omega m-2 beta Q+3 gamma S)
             = sum_j |c_j|² (D_j-D_l-beta Q+2 gamma S),
using the tested stationary equation. Every D_j<=D_l, including j=-l.

The repository's virial bound S<=sqrt(3 beta²/(32 gamma)) m Q/4
holds for this profile. Under the stated strict mass condition
we get -beta Q+2 gamma S<0 since Q>0. Hence the quadratic form is
strictly negative on the entire nonzero subspace. This proves the
constrained Morse index lower bound 4l. Removal of exact phase and
translation zero modes cannot decrease this negative dimension:
project the subspace off the finite-dimensional kernel directions;
the projection is injective on it and preserves its quadratic form.
A negative second variation along a mass-normalized curve excludes
local constrained minimality.

For l<0 use complex conjugation. The strict mass bound simplifies
to m<8 sqrt(2/(3 gamma)), since beta>0. No exact Morse index or
spectral count is claimed. The positive-frequency result makes the
Hessian's essential threshold positive, but Hamiltonian-Krein index
arguments still require actual spectral and constraint data. In
particular an even lower bound on negative directions is not an
odd-index instability theorem.

## 5. Dynamics within the angular symmetry class

The original flow preserves X_l by uniqueness and invariance under
the same circle action. Compactness of minimizing sequences just
proved gives Lyapunov stability of the full X_l-minimizing set
relative to initial data in X_l, by the conservation and mass
normalization argument of F01 (normalization preserves X_l).
If one phase orbit is isolated within this set, the same barrier
argument as in GC01IndividualOrbit proves individual stability
relative to X_l. Neither statement treats arbitrary H² perturbations.
The negative directions in section 4 are outside X_l, making the
distinction essential. No isolated phase orbit is established here.

## 6. Lean scope and prior art

GC01VortexEstimates.lean encodes the scalar estimates on the repository's
H²: positive frequency from the two precise scalar identities,
strict nonlinear gap, kinetic competitor implying non-minimality,
angular kinetic gap algebra, and negative angular coefficients.
It does NOT yet encode the angular-equivariant Sobolev space,
compactness construction, polar integration, weak Euler-Lagrange
identification or actual constrained Hessian dimension. These are
analytical arguments only. A separate CI target checks the candidate
scalar module and its exact axiom roots without relying on the prior
individual-orbit module.

Compare Feng and Stanislavova, On the vortices for the nonlinear
Schrödinger equation in higher dimensions (2018),
https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2017.0189, for equivariant variational
construction and the distinction between symmetry-restricted and
unrestricted stability in second-order NLS. Compare Bonheure et al.,
https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1154138, and the fourth-order solitary-wave
stability work https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09268. These references are
related, not a verified priority or novelty determination.

Remaining decisive problem: for the concrete vortices above,
construct the angular-pair linearized Hamiltonian operators, compute
their negative and Krein indices including phase/translation zero
modes, prove or disprove unstable eigenvalues, and verify nonlinear
escape in unrestricted H². The full original GC01 remains open.
