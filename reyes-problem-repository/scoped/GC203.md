# GC203: Degree obstruction to single-component deformation

**Precise candidate target v1, 2026-10-30.** Historical prompt: [GC203](../problems/08-topology-multicomponent/GC203.md). No claim of novel open status or Lean certification.

## Model and exact mathematical question

Let \(\Psi:S^3\to S^3\subset\mathbb C^2\) be a smooth unit two-component field, with topological degree \(\deg\Psi\in\mathbb Z\). Call a field single-component if \(\Psi(x)=(e^{i\theta(x)},0)\). Decide whether any degree-nonzero \(\Psi\) can be continuously homotoped through unit fields to a single-component map. Conversely determine whether \(\deg\Psi=0\) suffices for such a homotopy.

## Resolution and safeguards

Use \(\pi_3(S^3)=\mathbb Z\), and note all maps into S¹ have zero degree. The fixed nonvanishing constraint is essential; permitting zeros changes the target topology.

## Review checklist

Verify target homotopy groups, regularity/topology, energy finiteness, domain and boundary assumptions, ordinary counterexamples and prior work. A topological invariant is not automatically a dynamical stability theorem.
