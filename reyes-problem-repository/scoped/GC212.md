## Working mathematical model

Topological benchmark: smooth `n:ℝ³→S²` approaching a constant at infinity (hence `S³→S²`) with finite Faddeev–Skyrme-type energy `∫(|∇n|²+Σ_{i<j}|∂ᵢn×∂ⱼn|²) dx`. For vector-wave dynamics, separately freeze the two-component H² cubic–quintic equation; do not identify the two models.

This is a **benchmark choice for a first rigorous attempt**. It does not silently change the original research question, imply an existing solution or assert that the benchmark is the only admissible physical model.

## Candidate mathematical question

For a stochastic geometric field flow with continuous nonvanishing sample paths, determine whether topological charge remains almost surely constant.

**Quantifiers and domain discipline:** Fix integer degree/Hopf invariant, finite-energy class, nonvanishing hypotheses, admissible homotopies and whether evolution is continuous. Distinguish classical topology from energy-constrained dynamics.

**Observable to define explicitly:** topological charge, energy gap, admissible path, zero hitting or spectral index. Provide the exact norm, parameter ranges, and dependence of any claimed constant, bound, rate or threshold on all inputs.

## First decisive result

Prove conservation and characterize breakdown when regularity fails.

**Valid negative outcomes:** A continuous homotopy, zero-mediated charge change, or a counterexample to the proposed energy barrier.

State at least one precisely checkable theorem or counterexample on the benchmark. If the desired reference state, operator or measurement does not exist, that nonvacuity obstruction must be resolved **before** treating any conditional conclusion as substantive.

## Mathematical objections and prior-art obligations

**October 30 audit:** If sample paths are jointly continuous and never vanish, topological charge is homotopy-invariant pathwise; make zero hitting and regularity loss the substantive problem.

**Specific conceptual correction.** Continuous nonvanishing sample paths preserve topological degree. The nontrivial question concerns zero-hitting or loss of regularity under precisely stated stochastic forcing.

Check the closest existing papers, Lean declarations and classical theorems before labeling the statement new or open. Give exact hypotheses for any borrowed theorem, and keep computational checks separate from formal proofs.
