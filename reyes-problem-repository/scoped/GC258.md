# GC258: Scalar versus vector two-band envelope scaling

**Model-complete research candidate v1 (2026-10-30)**. Original: [GC258](../problems/11-physical-emergence-multiscale/GC258.md). Mathematical well-definedness and prior-art novelty are separate review gates.

## Exact microscopic/continuum target

Let \(i\partial_t(a,b)^T=H_\varepsilon(k)(a,b)^T\) with \(H_\varepsilon(k)=\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon^2k^2&\varepsilon^p g\\\varepsilon^p g&1+\varepsilon^q\delta+\varepsilon^2k^2\end{pmatrix}\), fixed g,δ∈[1/2,2], p,q∈{1,2,3,4}. On times t≤ε^-2 and initial (a,b)=(1,0), classify which p,q yield a decoupled scalar approximation \(a(t)\) with \(\sup_t|b(t)|\to0\) and which exhibit O(1) mode conversion.

## Decisive test and caveat

The finite-dimensional two-band unitary system is fixed and exactly solvable by matrix exponentiation. 'Fast mode elimination' must be tied to detuning relative to coupling, not to labels alone.

## Review conditions

Verify dimensional units, Fourier and interpolation conventions, existence of relevant solutions, quantifier/limit order and matched error estimate. A formal limiting expansion by itself is not a convergent approximation theorem.
