## Working mathematical model

Benchmark lattice: `q̈_j=ε⁻²(q_{j+1}−2q_j+q_{j−1})−V'(q_j)`, `j∈ℤ`, `V(s)=s²/2−s⁴/4+s⁶/6`, `ε∈(0,1]`, initially finite-energy compactly supported data. Vector/optical/3D variants need an independently specified microscopic Hamiltonian.

This is a **benchmark choice for a first rigorous attempt**. It does not silently change the original research question, imply an existing solution or assert that the benchmark is the only admissible physical model.

## Candidate mathematical question

Determine whether anisotropic microscopic coupling generates a nontrivial curvature-dependent effective operator in the continuum limit.

**Quantifiers and domain discipline:** Freeze interpolation, rescaled initial data, observation time `0≤t≤T/ε` (fixed T) and a norm for the error as ε→0. Identify hypotheses yielding uniform bounds.

**Observable to define explicitly:** effective coefficient, ε-error, long-time uniformity or lattice-continuum discrepancy. Provide the exact norm, parameter ranges, and dependence of any claimed constant, bound, rate or threshold on all inputs.

## First decisive result

Derive the operator and give a convergence or homogenization theorem.

**Valid negative outcomes:** Resonance, failure of uniform regularity, or noncommuting parameter limits.

State at least one precisely checkable theorem or counterexample on the benchmark. If the desired reference state, operator or measurement does not exist, that nonvacuity obstruction must be resolved **before** treating any conditional conclusion as substantive.

## Mathematical objections and prior-art obligations

**October 30 audit:** Define geometric curvature term; anisotropic stiffness alone does not uniquely determine a curvature operator.
