# GC299: Photon-loss and work bounds for resetting a one-photon memory

**Device-level mathematical candidate v1, 2026-10-30.** [Original GC299](../problems/12-photonic-wave-computing/GC299.md). The device model is idealized and **not** an experimentally validated optical implementation.

## Explicit physical abstraction and quantified mathematical target

A classical stored bit is equiprobable and encoded by vacuum |0⟩ or one photon |1⟩ of energy hν. Restrict reset protocols to a zero-temperature amplitude-damping channel which loses the stored photon to an unobserved environment with probability q∈[0,1], and otherwise leaves the one-photon state, with no active drive. Output should be vacuum with error probability at most p∈[0,1/2). Determine the minimum q and mean photons emitted per reset. Separately compare the entropy decrease with the Landauer work bound k_BT[ln2−h₂(p)ln2] for a **thermal** bath at T>0 and explain why the two bath models cannot be conflated.

## Proof or counterexample acceptance

The amplitude-damping model gives final error (1−q)/2, hence q≥1−2p and emitted photon mean q/2. It does not establish a universal photon-loss lower bound for energy-recovering reversible reset protocols.

## Scientific and formalization boundary

Prove all logical implications for the stated inputs, component transfer functions, limits and error models. Note any already elementary result rather than declaring a novel open conjecture. A mathematical truth table does not certify fabricated hardware; any Lean statement must match this model exactly.
