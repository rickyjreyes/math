# GC65: Swift–Hohenberg inertial-manifold existence

**Version:** scoped mathematical target v1 (2026-10-30). **Readiness:** precise candidate, not literature-certified open, not proven, and not claimed Lean-verified. Original draft: [GC65](../problems/01-wave-dynamics/GC65.md). Shared definitions: [COMMON_MODELS](COMMON_MODELS.md).

## Exact mathematical target

Let \(u_t=\mu u-(1+\Delta)^2u-u^3\) on the square torus \(\mathbb T^2=(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2\), with \(\mu=2\), real initial data \(H^2\). For each integer \(N\ge1\), let \(P_N\) be the \(L^2\)-orthogonal projection onto Fourier modes \(|k|\le N\), and \(Q_N=I-P_N\). Determine whether **there exist** \(N\) and a globally Lipschitz \(\Phi:P_NH^2\to Q_NH^2\) such that \(\mathcal M=\{p+\Phi(p)\}\) is forward invariant and, for every bounded subset \(B\subset H^2\), there are \(C_B,\lambda>0\) with \(\operatorname{dist}_{H^2}(S(t)B,\mathcal M)\le C_B e^{-\lambda t}\) for all \(t\ge0\).

## What answers it

A proof must exhibit N, Phi, invariance and exponential tracking. A disproof must exclude every N under these precise choices. The finite-dimensional attractor alone is insufficient.
